// METHODOLOGY

================================================================================================================

We work from public records: county procurement files, state grant agreements, legislative funding requests, property records, corporate filings, and campaign finance data. We request what is not already published under Chapter 119, Florida Statutes.

Every factual claim in a report carries one of three tags. Verified means we have the primary source document. Reported means it comes from credible news coverage. Inferred means it is a logical conclusion from verified facts that we have not independently confirmed, and we say so in the text.

Source documents are archived. Each report ends with a methodology note listing what it is based on, and reports are updated as new records come in.

// VERIFICATION LAB

================================================================================================================

Deepfakes and edited media travel fast, especially around elections and local fights. Our verification lab checks images, video, and audio before we report on them, and we will look at material readers send us.

CHECK 01

Provenance

Where did it first appear, who posted it, and does an earlier or original version exist?

CHECK 02

File and metadata

What the file itself says about when, where, and how it was made or edited.

CHECK 03

Image and frame review

Lighting, edges, shadows, text, and frame-by-frame signs of editing or generation.

CHECK 04

Audio review

Voice, background, and splice points in clips that claim to record a real person.

Seen something that looks off? Send the link or file to ethan@lucideragroup.com and tell us where you found it.

// PUBLIC RECORDS AND THE SUNSHINE LAW

================================================================================================================

Under Florida's public records law, Chapter 119, any person can ask to inspect or copy records held by a government agency. You do not have to give a reason. A request can be made in person, by phone, or in writing, though writing leaves a record of what you asked for and when.

Name the agency, describe the records as specifically as you can (dates, names, subjects, document numbers), and send the request to the agency's records custodian. Agencies may charge for copies and for large requests that take extensive staff time, so it is fair to ask for a cost estimate first.

Not sure where to start? Ask us. We will help you figure out which agency holds a record and how to word the request. If you think there is a request we should file ourselves, tell us that too.
